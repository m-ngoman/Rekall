Privacy Policy

Rekall · last updated 24 September 2026

Rekall is a small study app run by one person. This policy says exactly what it stores, why, and who else sees it. It is written to be read, not to be survived.

What Rekall stores

  • Your Google account details — your Google account ID, email address, name and profile picture URL. Used to sign you in and to keep your data separate from everyone else's. Rekall never sees or stores your Google password.
  • What you upload or write — photos and PDFs of your notes, kept as files, along with the text an AI model read out of them, and any notes you type straight into Rekall. A photo you attach to a tutor message is sent with that message but not kept.
  • Your study material — decks, cards, categories and exam dates you create or generate.
  • Your review history — for each answer: what you typed, whether it was graded for you or you rated your own recall, the grade, what the model said you missed, and when. This is what makes the scheduling work; without it Rekall cannot tell you what to study.
  • Tutor conversations — the messages you and the tutor exchange, the cards you've asked to go over with it, and a short profile the tutor keeps of how you study, so it does not start from nothing each time. The profile holds what the tutor has noticed across sessions and anything you write in it yourself, and you can read and edit all of it in the app. The tutor writes it from brief observations picked out of your conversations, which are stored alongside it.
  • Your settings — theme and accent colour, study limits and scheduling, grading strictness, which AI features are switched on, and the tutor's style, voice and microphone settings, including any instructions you write for it.
  • Your billing state — whether a plan is active, when it runs out, how many voice hours and card-generation pages you have left, a ledger of what you have bought and used, and an ID linking you to your payment record. Rekall never sees or stores your card details.
  • Problem reports — if you report a card as looking wrong, the report keeps a copy of the card's question, answer and subtopic, its deck's name, and how many times it had been reviewed. That copy stays with the report even if the card is later edited or deleted, so that the report still says what was wrong.

What Rekall does not do

No advertising. No third-party analytics or tracking pixels. No selling or sharing your data with anyone for marketing. No profiling beyond what is needed to schedule your cards and run the tutor. Rekall does count its own use — a row recording that a review happened, or a tutor turn, or how many pages went into making cards, or how many characters were spoken — so it can meter voice credits and the daily allowances, and show the person running it how busy the app is. Those rows hold no card, note or conversation text.

Who else sees your data

Rekall cannot generate cards, grade answers or speak without sending data to other companies. This is the part most policies bury, so plainly. The AI models are reached through OpenRouter, which passes each request to the company whose model answers it: at present Anthropic (Claude) for the tutor, for making cards and for reading your uploads, and Google (Gemini) for grading answers and for keeping your profile up to date.

  • Photos and PDFs you upload are sent to an AI provider to be read — into the text of your notes when you add them, and into cards when you generate from them. When you make cards from notes you've already saved, their text is sent the same way; when you make them from a topic, what you wrote about the topic is sent, along with any notes filed under the deck you chose. Do not upload anything you would not want a third party to process — including other people's private information.
  • Your typed answers are sent to an AI provider to be graded, with the card's question and answer. Answers you rate yourself are not sent anywhere.
  • Your tutor conversation is sent to an AI provider — your messages, any photo you attach, and your profile, together with the cards you keep missing or asked to go over and your upcoming exams, which is how it knows what to help with. The conversation so far is re-sent each turn, because that is the only way the model can follow the thread. Every fourth message you send, the recent part of the conversation is also sent to a second, cheaper model, which picks out anything worth remembering about how you study and updates your profile. It is sent your profile too, the observations it has picked out before, and the lines you have taken out of your profile, so that it doesn't write them back. You can switch it off in Settings.
  • In voice mode, your speech is sent to a speech-to-text provider, Deepgram, and the tutor's replies are sent to a text-to-speech provider, Cartesia or Inworld, to be spoken.
  • If you buy anything, your email address and name are sent to Stripe, which handles the payment, with an ID that ties the payment to your Rekall account. Your card is entered on Stripe's own page and never reaches Rekall.
  • Google signs you in, and serves the app's fonts and your profile picture, so your browser contacts Google when the page loads.

These providers process the data to return a result. Rekall does not authorise them to use it for anything else. Providers may change; this page will be updated when they do.

Cookies and browser storage

One cookie, rekall_session, which keeps you signed in. Your browser's local storage keeps your theme and accent colour, so the page doesn't flash the wrong colours while it loads, the last colour you picked from the colour wheel, and the note editor's font and text size. Nothing is used to follow you anywhere else.

Where it lives

On a virtual server rented from OVHcloud in Beauharnois, Quebec, so your data is stored in Canada. Files you upload are stored on that server's disk; everything else is in a database on the same server. OVHcloud provides the machine; it does not use or read your data. A nightly backup is kept on the same server for 14 days, and OVHcloud keeps its own daily copy of the whole server.

How long it is kept

Until you delete it or ask for your account to be removed. Deleting a note, deck or card removes it, including the file on disk — except that a card you reported stays copied in its report (see above). Tutor conversations can't yet be deleted from inside the app; ask, and they will be. The same goes for the observations your profile is written from, and for the lines you take out of it, which are kept word for word so the tutor doesn't write them back. To close your account, email the address below — it is done by hand, covering your uploaded files as well as everything in the database, and you get a confirmation when it is finished. The usage counts described above are kept afterwards, with nothing linking them to you.

Backups are kept for 14 days, so anything deleted can survive in one for up to two weeks after it is gone from Rekall itself.

What you can ask for

A copy of your data, correction of anything wrong, or complete deletion. Your decks and cards can be downloaded from Settings at any time, as CSV or JSON; for anything else, email the address below and you will get a straight answer from a person.

Children

Rekall is not intended for children under 13, and accounts are not knowingly created for them.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected or who receives it, the date at the top changes and anyone with an account is told.
